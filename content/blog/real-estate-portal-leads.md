Property portal leads are distributed to several brokers simultaneously, which turns lead response into a race rather than a conversation. The broker who replies first typically secures the site visit, and the site visit is what produces the deal. Since brokers spend most of their day at properties and unable to answer, automated instant response is the single highest-return change available to a brokerage.

## Key takeaways

- Portal leads go to **multiple brokers at once**. You are always in a race you did not agree to enter.
- Brokers are structurally unavailable — **at site visits, driving, or with a client** — exactly when leads arrive.
- Qualifying **budget, configuration, locality and timeline** before a visit prevents wasted afternoons.
- The **post-visit follow-up** is the second largest leak and the easiest to fix.
- You paid for the lead. Not answering it in 60 seconds is paying for a competitor's deal.

## The portal lead race

When a buyer submits an enquiry on a property portal, that enquiry does not go only to you. It is distributed to several listings and several brokers, often within the same minute.

So the buyer's phone is now receiving calls and messages from four people about the same requirement. What happens next is predictable:

1. The first broker to make meaningful contact starts a conversation
2. That broker learns the requirement and offers matching options
3. The buyer, now engaged with someone helpful, agrees to a site visit
4. Brokers two, three and four call four hours later and reach a buyer who has already committed their Saturday

The deal is not usually won on better inventory or a better commission. **It is won on being first.**

This is the [speed-to-lead](https://arifwork.com/blog/speed-to-lead) principle in its most literal form — nowhere else is the competition so simultaneous and so visible.

## Why brokers structurally cannot be first

This is not a discipline problem. It is a job-shape problem.

| When leads arrive | Where the broker is |
| --- | --- |
| Weekday afternoons | Showing a property to another client |
| Evenings | Driving between sites in traffic |
| Weekends — peak lead volume | Conducting back-to-back site visits |
| Late night | Asleep |
| During a negotiation | Cannot take a call without damaging the deal |

A broker cannot pause a site visit to answer a portal enquiry without insulting the client in front of them. So the enquiry waits. And while it waits, three competitors are calling it.

The only resolution is that something other than the broker answers immediately.

## The four questions to ask before any site visit

Site visits are expensive — fuel, traffic, hours, and the client you could not show instead. Qualify first:

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| **Budget range?** | Whether your inventory fits at all |
| **Configuration and carpet area?** | Which properties to shortlist |
| **Preferred localities?** | Whether the enquiry matches your operating area |
| **Timeline and loan status?** | Buyer versus browser — the most important distinction |

Loan status is the most underused question in Indian brokerage. "Pre-approved" and "haven't looked into it yet" describe two completely different people who will consume the same number of your Sundays.

## The second leak: post-visit silence

A broker shows three properties on Sunday. The buyer says they will think about it. Then nothing — from either side.

The broker does not chase, because chasing feels pushy in a relationship business. The buyer keeps looking with someone else.

A structured, low-pressure sequence:

| When | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Same evening | "Thanks for your time today — which of the three felt closest?" |
| Day 2 | Floor plans, documents and photographs of the favourite |
| Day 4 | One new matching property that just came up |
| Day 8 | "Any questions on the paperwork or loan side?" |
| Day 15 | Market note on the locality — useful, not salesy |
| Monthly | New inventory matching their stated brief |

Property decisions take months. The broker who is still gently present in month three closes the deal, and presence is a scheduling problem, not a personality trait.

---

## The third leak: your own past clients

A client bought through you three years ago. You have not spoken since. They are now upgrading, or their brother is buying, or they want to rent out that same flat — and they are talking to whoever is in front of them.

A light quarterly touch — a genuine locality price update, not a sales message — keeps you in the frame for the transaction that eventually comes. For a broker, past clients and their referrals are the cheapest deal flow available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do property portal leads convert so poorly?

Because each enquiry is sent to several brokers at once and most respond hours later. The buyer engages with whoever replies first and commits their viewing time to that broker, so the remaining brokers are effectively competing for a decision that has already been made.

### How fast should a broker respond to a portal lead?

Within a minute wherever possible. Portal leads are distributed simultaneously to multiple brokers, so response time is the primary competitive variable — more decisive than inventory, pricing or experience for that first contact.

### How do I qualify a property buyer before a site visit?

Ask four things: budget range, configuration and carpet area, preferred localities, and timeline including loan status. Loan status in particular separates serious buyers from browsers, and asking it at first contact prevents Sundays spent showing properties to people who cannot transact yet.

### What should I send a buyer after a site visit?

A short message the same evening asking which property felt closest, then documents for their favourite, one new matching listing, a check on loan and paperwork questions, and periodic locality updates. Property decisions take months, and most brokers stop following up long before the decision is made.

### Can automation handle real estate leads without damaging trust?

Yes, when it is transparent and limited to speed. An assistant that identifies itself honestly, gathers requirements and books a visit protects the relationship, because the alternative is silence. Negotiation, recommendation and closing must remain entirely human.
