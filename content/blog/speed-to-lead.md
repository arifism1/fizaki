Speed to lead is the time between a prospect contacting your business and your first meaningful reply. It is one of the strongest predictors of whether that lead converts — research consistently shows conversion odds fall sharply after the first five minutes and collapse after the first hour. For most local businesses, cutting response time is cheaper and more effective than increasing ad spend.

## Key takeaways

- **Speed to lead** measures the gap between enquiry and first real reply, not first automated acknowledgement.
- Conversion odds drop steeply within minutes. The Harvard Business Review lead response study found the first five minutes to be disproportionately decisive.
- Roughly 78% of customers buy from whoever responds first, which means speed often beats price, brand and quality of pitch.
- Most Indian local businesses reply in hours, not minutes — which is why replying in 60 seconds is currently a genuine competitive advantage.
- You cannot solve this by trying harder. You solve it with automation that responds while your team works.

---

## What is speed to lead?

Speed to lead is the elapsed time between a prospective customer submitting an enquiry — a call, form fill, WhatsApp message, Instagram DM or ad click — and your business making its first substantive response.

Two clarifications matter:

1. **An auto-reply saying "we have received your enquiry" is not a response.** It does not qualify, does not answer the question, and does not book anything. It buys you almost nothing.
2. **The clock starts when the customer acts, not when you notice.** A lead that arrives at 9 PM and gets answered at 10 AM has a response time of thirteen hours, not one minute of effort.

---

## What does the research actually say?

The most cited work here is the Harvard Business Review analysis *The Short Life of Online Sales Leads*, which examined thousands of firms and found that those attempting contact within an hour were far more likely to qualify a lead than those who waited longer — and that the advantage was concentrated in the first five minutes. *[Verify + link the HBR article.]*

The often-quoted **five-minute rule** derives from this body of research: contact a lead within five minutes and your odds of a meaningful conversation multiply relative to contacting them even thirty minutes later.

From the buyer's side, the widely cited figure is that **around 78% of customers purchase from the company that responds to their enquiry first**. *[Verify + link source.]*

Both findings point at the same underlying human behaviour: **enquiry intent decays fast.** The moment someone reaches out, they are at peak motivation. Every minute after that, other things reclaim their attention — and competitors get a chance.

## Why speed beats budget

Consider two businesses spending the same on ads.

|  | Business A | Business B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend | ₹50,000 | ₹50,000 |
| Leads generated | 100 | 100 |
| Average response time | 4 hours | 60 seconds |
| Realistic conversion rate | 8% | 20% |
| Customers won | 8 | 20 |
| Effective cost per customer | ₹6,250 | ₹2,500 |

Business B did not buy more traffic. It captured more of the traffic it already paid for. To match Business B's results, Business A would need to **spend two and a half times more** — an enormous, permanent, recurring cost — to fix something that automation solves once.

This is the single most important idea in local business marketing, and almost nobody acts on it: **the cheapest leads you will ever buy are the ones you have already paid for and failed to answer.**

## What is a good lead response time?

| Response time | Grade | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 minute | Excellent | You are almost always first. The lead is still on their phone. |
| 1–5 minutes | Very good | Within the research-backed window. Strong conversion. |
| 5–30 minutes | Acceptable | Losing to faster competitors on contested leads. |
| 30 min – 4 hours | Poor | The buying moment has passed for most enquiries. |
| Over 4 hours | Critical | You are effectively re-marketing to a stranger. |

Most Indian local service businesses sit in the bottom two rows — not through carelessness, but because the owner is the salesperson and the salesperson is busy delivering the service.

That is also the opportunity. In a market where the average competitor replies in hours, a business that replies in seconds wins contested leads by default.

## Why "just reply faster" never works

Every business owner already knows they should reply faster. Telling them again changes nothing, because the constraint is not willingness.

The constraint is physical:

- You cannot answer a call while you are inside a patient's mouth.
- You cannot check Instagram DMs while spotting a deadlift.
- You cannot reply at 11 PM because you are asleep.
- You cannot be first when a portal sells the same lead to four brokers simultaneously.

Effort does not solve a physics problem. **Only automation does.**

## How to get to a 60-second response time

1. **Measure your current baseline.** For two weeks, log the timestamp of each enquiry and each first reply. The average will be worse than you expect. This number is your before.
2. **Consolidate every channel into one inbox.** Calls, website forms, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs and ad lead forms should land in a single place. Leads die in channels nobody checks.
3. **Automate the first reply.** An [AI receptionist](https://fizaki.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-receptionist) sends a real, contextual response within seconds — not an acknowledgement, but an actual conversation opener that asks the qualifying question.
4. **Let the AI qualify and book.** The goal is not to hold the lead until a human is free. It is to complete the transaction the lead came for: an appointment, a site visit, a trial.
5. **Hand off cleanly to a human.** Anything advisory, sensitive or high-value routes to a person, with the conversation history attached so nothing is repeated.
6. **Track it permanently.** Response time should be a metric you review weekly, alongside spend and revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good speed to lead?

Under five minutes is the research-backed threshold, and under 60 seconds is best in class. Beyond thirty minutes, conversion rates drop sharply because the prospect has usually contacted a competitor in the meantime.

### Does an automatic reply count as responding?

Only if it does real work. A generic "thanks, we'll get back to you" does not hold attention. A reply that answers the customer's question, asks a qualifying question or offers an appointment slot does count, because it continues the conversation rather than pausing it.

### Is speed more important than the quality of the reply?

They are not in conflict. Speed gets you the conversation; quality wins it. But a perfect reply sent four hours late usually arrives after the customer has already booked elsewhere, so speed is the prerequisite.

### How do I improve response time without hiring?

Automate the first response. An AI system can answer missed calls, WhatsApp messages and DMs within seconds, qualify the lead and book an appointment, escalating to a human only when the conversation needs one.

### Does this apply to expensive, considered purchases too?

Yes, and often more so. For high-value purchases like solar installations or property, buyers contact several suppliers at once. The first responder frames the entire comparison and is usually still in the running at the end.
