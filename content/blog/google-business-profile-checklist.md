To rank higher in Google's local map pack, your Google Business Profile needs a precise primary category, complete and consistent business information, a steady flow of recent reviews with replies, regularly refreshed photos, and active use of Posts, Products and Q&A. Local ranking is driven by three factors Google names openly: relevance, distance and prominence — and you can influence two of the three.

## Key takeaways

- Google ranks local results on **relevance, distance and prominence**. You control relevance and prominence.
- Your **primary category** is the single highest-impact field. Get it exactly right.
- **Completeness matters** — profiles with every field filled outperform sparse ones consistently.
- **Review velocity and recency** matter as much as total count.
- Most Indian local profiles lose visibility to three fixable problems: wrong category, inconsistent NAP, and dead photos.

---

## How does Google decide local rankings?

Google states publicly that local results are ranked on three factors:

| Factor | What it means | Can you influence it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relevance** | How well your profile matches the search | Yes, strongly |
| **Distance** | How far you are from the searcher | No |
| **Prominence** | How well-known and well-regarded you are | Yes, over time |

Since distance is fixed, everything below is about maximising relevance and prominence.

## The 20-point checklist

### Foundations

**1. Claim and verify the profile.** Unverified profiles rank poorly and can be edited by the public. Complete verification even if it requires a postcard or video.

**2. Choose the correct primary category.** This is the most influential single field. Be specific: "Dental clinic" not "Doctor"; "Solar energy contractor" not "Contractor". Check what the top-ranking competitor in your area uses.

**3. Add every relevant secondary category** — but only genuinely relevant ones. Irrelevant categories dilute relevance rather than expanding it.

**4. Make your business name exactly your real business name.** Do not stuff keywords into it. "Sharma Dental" is fine; "Sharma Dental – Best Dentist in Pune, Implants, RCT" violates Google's naming guidelines and risks suspension.

**5. Ensure NAP consistency.** Your Name, Address and Phone must match exactly across your website, Google, Justdial, Practo, IndiaMART, Facebook and every directory. Inconsistency actively suppresses local rankings. Pick one format and standardise everywhere.

### Information completeness

**6. Set accurate opening hours**, including special hours for Indian public holidays and festivals. Wrong hours generate the worst possible review — "they were closed when Google said open".

**7. Write a keyword-informed but human business description.** 750 characters. Lead with what you do, who you serve and where. Do not keyword-stuff.

**8. Add your service area** if you travel to customers — essential for solar installers, brokers and interior designers.

**9. List every service or product individually** in the Services section. Each entry is an additional relevance signal. A dental clinic should list root canal, implants, aligners, whitening, cleaning and extraction separately, each with a short description.

**10. Add attributes** — wheelchair accessible, online appointments, women-owned, parking available, accepts UPI. These surface in filters and increase relevance for qualified searches.

**11. Link your website and add an appointment link.** If you have online booking, add the booking URL. This drives conversions directly from the profile.

### Content and activity

**12. Upload high-quality photos, and keep uploading.** Exterior, interior, team, work in progress, completed results. Profiles with fresh, regular photo uploads perform better than those with a one-time dump. Aim for a few new photos monthly.

**13. Add a short video** if you can. Very few local competitors do, which makes it a cheap differentiator.

**14. Publish Google Posts weekly.** Offers, updates, events, new services. Posts signal an active profile and occupy additional space in your listing.

**15. Seed and answer the Q&A section.** You are allowed to post questions and answer them yourself. Add the ten questions customers actually ask — parking, timings, payment methods, whether appointments are needed — and answer them clearly. Unanswered questions get answered by strangers, often wrongly.

### Reviews

**16. Build a steady review flow.** Velocity and recency matter, not just count. See [how to get Google reviews on autopilot](https://arifwork.com/blog/get-more-google-reviews).

**17. Reply to every review**, positive and negative, within a few days.

**18. Never buy reviews or gate them by sentiment.** Both are policy violations with real consequences.

### Technical and measurement

**19. Add LocalBusiness schema to your website** with details matching your profile exactly — name, address, phone, hours, geo-coordinates. This reinforces the entity relationship between your site and your listing.

**20. Track calls and direction requests** in Google Business Profile insights. And critically, **make sure the calls those listings generate are actually answered** — a highly optimised profile that drives calls into a phone nobody picks up simply converts your local SEO investment into [lead leakage](https://arifwork.com/blog/lead-leakage-local-business).

## The three mistakes that quietly kill Indian local rankings

**Wrong primary category.** Extremely common and enormously costly. A physiotherapy clinic categorised as "Doctor" competes in the wrong pool entirely. Audit this first.

**Inconsistent NAP across Indian directories.** Justdial, IndiaMART, Sulekha and Practo listings created years ago with an old number or a differently formatted address actively confuse Google. Audit and correct them.

**A dead profile.** No new photos, no posts, no recent reviews, unanswered questions. Google's prominence signal rewards activity. A profile last touched in 2023 looks abandoned.

## A realistic monthly maintenance routine

| Frequency | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly | Publish one Google Post; reply to new reviews |
| Fortnightly | Upload 2–3 new photos |
| Monthly | Check insights; add a Q&A entry; verify hours |
| Quarterly | Audit categories and services; check NAP across all directories |
| Annually | Refresh the business description and cover photo |

Thirty minutes a week sustains this. Most competitors will not do it, which is precisely why it works.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I rank higher on Google Maps?

Optimise for the two ranking factors you control: relevance and prominence. Set a precise primary category, complete every profile field, list services individually, publish posts and photos regularly, and build a steady stream of recent reviews with replies. Distance from the searcher is the third factor and cannot be changed.

### What is the most important part of a Google Business Profile?

The primary category. It determines which searches your profile is eligible to appear in at all. After that, review quantity and recency, and overall profile completeness, have the largest influence.

### Can I put keywords in my Google business name?

No. Your profile name must be your actual business name as used in the real world. Adding keywords or locations violates Google's guidelines and can lead to your listing being suspended or edited.

### How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

About once a week is a practical cadence. Posts demonstrate that the profile is active and occupy additional space in your listing, which improves click-through even where the direct ranking effect is modest.

### Does NAP consistency really matter in India?

Yes, and it is one of the most commonly neglected factors. Old Justdial, Sulekha, IndiaMART and Practo listings with outdated phone numbers or differently formatted addresses send conflicting signals to Google and suppress local rankings. Standardise one exact format everywhere.

### Should I answer my own questions in the Q&A section?

Yes. Google permits businesses to post and answer their own questions, and it is far better than leaving common questions unanswered or answered incorrectly by members of the public.
